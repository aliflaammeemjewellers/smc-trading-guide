01 · Core map

The SMC framework

SMC is a price-action framework. No single label guarantees a trade; context, execution and risk management matter.

SMC

Market Structure

Swing highs/lows, trend, range and the sequence of HH/HL or LH/LL. Start with the higher timeframe.

SMC

BOS · Break of Structure

A meaningful break in the direction of the established structure. Use displacement and context to avoid weak breaks.

SMC

CHoCH / MSS

A change in short-term structure that may signal a shift. A confirmation model—not a prediction by itself.

SMC

Liquidity

Areas where orders/stops may cluster: equal highs/lows, old highs/lows, session extremes and obvious swing points.

SMC

Liquidity Sweep

Price trades through a visible liquidity pool and then rejects or reverses. Look for confirmation after the sweep.

SMC

Inducement

A tempting minor structure or liquidity point that can draw participation before price reaches the more important pool.

SMC

Order Block

A price zone associated with the final opposing candle/area before a strong displacement. Quality depends on context.

SMC

Breaker Block

A former order-block area that fails and can later act from the opposite side after a structural shift.

SMC

Mitigation

A return into a prior zone where unfilled orders or positions may be addressed before continuation/reversal.

SMC

Fair Value Gap (FVG)

A three-candle imbalance where price moves efficiently, leaving a gap between wicks of candles 1 and 3.

SMC

Inverse FVG

An FVG that fails and can be retested from the opposite side as a reaction area.

SMC

Displacement

A strong, decisive price expansion showing urgency. Often used to validate a break or zone.

SMC

Premium / Discount

Divide a dealing range at equilibrium (50%). Long ideas are commonly sought in discount; shorts in premium.

SMC

OTE

Optimal Trade Entry: a retracement framework often using the 62–79% area of a selected dealing range. Not a guarantee.

SMC

Dealing Range

A defined swing high-to-low range used to frame equilibrium, premium/discount and targets.

SMC

Session Liquidity

Asia, London and New York session highs/lows can become reference points for intraday liquidity.

SMC

SMT Divergence

Relative divergence between correlated markets/instruments, used as supporting context rather than a standalone signal.

SMC

POI · Point of Interest

A confluence zone where structure, liquidity, imbalance, session context or other factors align.

03 · Liquidity

Where orders may sit

Liquidity is a map of obvious levels, not a promise that price must take them.

Buy-side vs sell-side liquidity

Buy-side: often above prior highs, equal highs and obvious resistance.
Sell-side: often below prior lows, equal lows and obvious support. A sweep can provide context for a reversal or continuation setup.

Equal Highs / Lows

Repeated extremes are visually obvious and can become liquidity references.

Internal / External

Internal liquidity sits inside a range; external liquidity sits beyond its major boundaries.

04 · Execution

Entry models

Use these as study templates. Define invalidation and position size before entering any live trade.

Liquidity sweep → displacement → retracement

1) Identify a meaningful liquidity pool. 2) Wait for price to sweep it. 3) Look for displacement and a structural shift. 4) Mark an FVG/OB. 5) Define entry, invalidation and target before execution.

BOS → imbalance retracement

1) Establish directional structure. 2) Confirm a meaningful BOS with displacement. 3) Mark the resulting FVG or relevant order-block area. 4) Wait for retracement. 5) Target opposing liquidity or a predefined objective.

Failed order block → breaker

1) Mark a significant OB. 2) Price decisively invalidates it. 3) Structure confirms the shift. 4) Watch the former zone on a retest. 5) Require reaction and defined invalidation.

Premium/discount + OTE + POI

1) Define a clean dealing range. 2) Locate equilibrium. 3) Combine premium/discount with an OB/FVG/structure level. 4) Use OTE only as confluence. 5) Wait for a lower-timeframe trigger.

05 · Process

Pre-trade checklist

A repeatable process is more useful than memorizing labels.

✓ Higher-timeframe context identified
✓ Meaningful swing structure mapped
✓ External liquidity marked
✓ Premium/discount or range context clear
✓ POI has real confluence
✓ Trigger/confirmation defined
✓ Invalidation level defined first
✓ Position size fits your risk rules
